You know what you intended to do. Moments later, you watch yourself do the opposite. What happens in that interval?

A map, not a doctrine

Three entrances to a difficult question.

Explore three different sources. They are not the same teaching—and their differences are part of what makes the question worth asking.

The central question

Why does intention fail to become action?

01 / SCRIPTUREThe conflict
in PaulOPEN SOURCE PATH ↗

In Romans 7:15–25, Paul describes the distance between willing the good and acting otherwise. His context is theological: sin, law, and the need for deliverance.

Read Romans 7 (USCCB) ↗

02 / FOURTH WAYThe changing
inner “I”OPEN SOURCE PATH ↗

Fourth Way literature describes shifting and sometimes conflicting inner “I”s. This is a concept within that tradition, not a verified clinical model.

It offers a different angle on the moment a reaction displaces an intention.

Nicoll: Psychological Commentaries ↗

03 / ATTENTIONThe observing
sideOPEN SOURCE PATH ↗

Nicoll's account of self-observation invites attention to reactions without instantly identifying with them. Separately, his The New Man offers a psychological reading of the Gospels.

Nicoll: The New Man (1950) ↗

The crucial distinction: placing Romans 7 beside the Fourth Way is an editorial analogy, not a historical equivalence, official Christian interpretation, or claim about the brain.
